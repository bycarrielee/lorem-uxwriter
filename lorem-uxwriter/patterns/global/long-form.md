# Long-form copy guidelines

Rules for writing long-form content across all products. Products can override by creating `patterns/{product}/long-form.md`.

---

## What long-form copy is for

Long-form pages explain a service, policy, or life situation in enough depth for users to understand their eligibility, options, and next steps.

---

## Core writing rules

### 1. Write for readers who skim
Most users scan before they read. They check headings, read the first sentence of each section, and look for their specific answer. Use headings, summary sentences, lists, and tables as navigation tools, not formatting decoration.

### 2. Frontload the most important information
Lead with the most important information. State the answer, then explain it. Do not spend more than two sentences explaining what the page covers; users who arrived via search already know the topic. Frontloading answers may also help AI search tools extract accurate responses when summarising or citing the page.

Frame the answer from the user's perspective: what they can do or get, not how the scheme works. Start with "You can" or "You are entitled to", not "The scheme provides" or "Employers must".

| Bad example | Good example |
| --- | --- |
| `Shared Parental Leave (SPL) is a scheme introduced as part of Singapore's efforts to support working parents and encourage shared caregiving responsibilities between both parents.` | `Shared Parental Leave (SPL) gives eligible parents up to 10 weeks of paid leave to share after a Singapore citizen child is born.` |
| `This page explains what happens when a loved one passes away and the steps you may need to take.` | `When someone dies in Singapore, the family usually needs to register the death, apply for a Grant of Probate or Letters of Administration, and notify relevant government agencies.` |
| `The Shared Parental Leave scheme gives parents a combined pool of leave to divide between them.` | `Parents can share up to 10 weeks of paid leave after their child is born.` |

### 3. Cut content that does not help the user act
Every sentence should help users understand their eligibility, options, or next steps. Background that does not change what the user knows or does should be cut.

Before publishing each section, ask: if this were removed, would the user be less able to decide or act? If not, remove it.

### 4. Use plain language
Write at a level accessible to non-specialists. Avoid legal language, bureaucratic phrasing, and unexplained acronyms. Government content is frequently translated and read by users whose first language is not English; avoid idioms and phrasing that relies on native-speaker familiarity.

| Bad example | Good example |
| --- | --- |
| `The quantum of leave is contingent upon the employee satisfying the requisite eligibility criteria.` | `The amount of leave you get depends on whether you qualify.` |
| `Intestate succession applies where the deceased has not executed a valid testamentary instrument.` | `If the person who died did not leave a will, their assets are distributed according to the Intestate Succession Act.` |

| Idiomatic | Plain |
| --- | --- |
| `You're in the clear if you've ticked all the boxes.` | `You qualify if you meet all the conditions.` |
| `Your application is in the pipeline.` | `Your application is being processed.` |
| `Once you're up and running, you can log in to check your status.` | `Once your account is set up, you can log in to check your status.` |

### 5. Be specific: use exact figures
Vague copy makes users contact the agency for clarification. Exact figures reduce misquotation when content is shared.

| Bad example | Good example |
| --- | --- |
| `You may be entitled to a period of paid leave.` | `You are entitled to up to 10 weeks of Shared Parental Leave to share with your co-parent.` |
| `The payment will be made within a reasonable timeframe.` | `Payment must be made within 14 days of the employee's return to work.` |
| `Your income must be below the qualifying threshold.` | `Your monthly household income must not exceed $3,000, or $1,000 per capita.` |

### 6. Keep sentences short
Aim for 15 to 20 words per sentence. Break long sentences at natural clause boundaries.

| Long sentence | Split version |
| --- | --- |
| `If the employee has not served the employer for at least 3 continuous months immediately before the birth of the child, they will not be eligible for Shared Parental Leave regardless of the child's citizenship status.` | `Employees must have worked for the employer for at least 3 continuous months before the child is born. If they have not, they do not qualify, even if the child is a Singapore citizen.` |

### 7. Use consistent terminology
Pick one term for each concept and use it throughout. Do not substitute synonyms for variety. It confuses users and breaks continuity when content is shared. Consistent terminology and definition sentences may also help AI search tools correctly identify and explain terms when summarising page content.

Introduce new terms with a definition sentence: `[Term] is [definition].`

| Inconsistent | Consistent |
| --- | --- |
| `passed away`, `deceased`, `the person who died`, `your loved one` (mixed throughout) | Use `the person who died` for procedural steps; `your loved one` is acceptable in empathetic framing |
| `leave`, `time off`, `absence` | Use `leave` throughout |
| `apply`, `submit`, `register` | Use the correct verb for each action; do not substitute |

| Weak | With definition sentence |
| --- | --- |
| `You may have heard of SPL.` | `Shared Parental Leave (SPL) is a government scheme that gives eligible parents up to 10 weeks of paid leave to share after a Singapore citizen child is born.` |
| `CPF plays a role here.` | `CPF (Central Provident Fund) savings are not part of a person's estate and are distributed separately according to a CPF nomination, not a will.` |

### 8. Open each section with a summary sentence
The first sentence of each section should summarise the whole section. Readers who scan get the key point; readers who read in depth get the context that follows. Summary sentences may also help AI search tools extract accurate, self-contained answers from individual sections.

| Without summary sentence | With summary sentence |
| --- | --- |
| `Employees must have worked for the employer for at least 3 months. The leave must be taken within 12 months of the child's birth. If the employee is on a fixed-term contract, the contract must not have ended before the leave is taken.` | `To qualify, employees must meet three conditions. They must have worked for the employer for at least 3 months, take the leave within 12 months of the child's birth, and still be employed when the leave is taken.` |

### 9. Use lists for conditions, steps, and options
Use a bulleted list when there are 3 or more parallel items. Use a numbered list for steps that must be followed in order. Use prose for context, explanation, and consequences.

**Use a bulleted list for eligibility conditions:**
> To qualify for Shared Parental Leave, the parent must:
> - have a Singapore citizen child
> - have been employed continuously for at least 3 months before the child is born
> - take the leave within 12 months of the child's birth

**Use a numbered list for sequential steps:**
> To register a death:
> 1. Obtain the Notice of Death from the attending doctor or hospital
> 2. Submit the Notice of Death at any SingPost branch or online via MyLegacy
> 3. Receive the Death Certificate, usually within 3 to 5 working days

**Use prose for explanation:**
> If the death occurred overseas, different procedures apply. The death may need to be registered in the country where it occurred before the Singapore authorities can be notified.

### 10. Use tables for comparisons and structured data
When presenting eligibility tiers, quantum amounts, or comparisons between options, use a table. Tables are scannable and machine-readable.

| Leave type | Who takes it | Duration | Paid by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Government-Paid Maternity Leave | Mother | 16 weeks | Government (employer reimburses) |
| Shared Parental Leave | Both parents (shared between them) | Up to 10 weeks | Government |
| Paternity Leave | Father | 2 weeks | Government |

### 11. Write self-contained paragraphs
Each paragraph should make sense without the surrounding context. Users often arrive mid-page via search or a shared link. A paragraph lifted out of context should still answer the question completely.

**Before (depends on context above):**
> This applies to the scenarios described in the previous section.

**After (self-contained):**
> Employers are required to pay Shared Parental Leave within 14 days of the employee's return to work. This applies regardless of whether the leave was taken in one block or in separate periods.

### 12. Match the tone to the subject
Government pages cover a wide range of human situations. Match the tone to the context.

| Context | Tone | What to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Benefits and entitlements | Practical, direct, neutral | Overly formal or legalistic |
| Life events (birth, marriage) | Warm but not effusive | Assuming emotional response (see states.md) |
| Loss and death | Calm, practical, compassionate | Clinical detachment or excessive sentiment |
| Eligibility and legal criteria | Precise, factual | Vagueness or hedging |

For sensitive topics such as death, illness, or relationship breakdown, acknowledge the situation briefly before moving to practical steps. Do not linger. The user came for information.

**Example (When Death Happens opening):**
> Losing someone is hard. This page covers the practical steps the family or next-of-kin will need to take, including registering the death, managing the person's CPF and assets, and notifying relevant agencies.

## Anti-patterns

| Copy | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Opening with policy rationale or history before the answer | Users need the answer first; context can follow |
| `Please note that eligibility is subject to change` (without a date or source) | Vague hedging; if criteria may change, link to the authoritative source |
| Using synonyms for key terms to avoid repetition | Creates inconsistency; repeat the term |
| Burying eligibility criteria in a paragraph of prose | Use a list; criteria must be scannable |
| Soft-pedalling thresholds: `around $3,000`, `approximately 4 weeks` | Use exact figures; approximations invite misquotation |
| Section headings like `Overview`, `Background`, `Introduction` | These do not match user search queries; use specific, descriptive headings |
| Ending a page without a next step or action | Users need to know what to do with the information they just read |
| Writing for the average case only | Government services cover edge cases; name the exceptions explicitly |
